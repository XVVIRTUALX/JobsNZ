Welcome to Work Orientation

Starting work means learning new skills, meeting new people and understanding what is expected in a workplace.

This JobsNZ orientation introduces some basic workplace behaviours, rights and responsibilities.

You are not expected to know everything on your first day.

Be reliable. Be willing to learn. Communicate. Work safely. Ask when you are unsure.

This orientation does not replace an employer's own workplace induction, job training, supervision or health and safety responsibilities.

1. Reliability & Attendance

Turn up when you have agreed to work and arrive ready to start.

If you are sick, delayed or unable to attend, communicate with your employer as soon as reasonably possible.

2. Communication & Learning

Listen carefully to instructions.

Ask questions when you do not understand something. Being new means there will be things you have not done before.

Be respectful, open to learning and responsive when someone is showing you how work should be done.

3. Work Safely

Take reasonable care of your own health and safety and avoid doing anything that could harm another person.

Follow reasonable workplace health and safety instructions, procedures and training.

Use required protective equipment correctly.

If you are unsure — stop and ask.

Never pretend to understand a task when you are uncertain how to perform it safely.

4. Instructions, Tools & Equipment

Follow lawful and reasonable instructions that relate to your work.

Only use tools, machinery or equipment that you are permitted and appropriately trained to use.

Look after workplace equipment and report damaged or unsafe equipment to the appropriate person.

5. Workplace Behaviour

Treat workmates, customers and other people respectfully.

Follow reasonable workplace policies covering behaviour, clothing, phones, email, social media and workplace property.

Protect confidential workplace information.

If you make a mistake, be honest and ask how to correct it. Mistakes can be part of learning.

6. Know Your Employment Rights

Starting work also means understanding your rights.

Employees have minimum employment rights covering matters such as employment agreements, pay, breaks, holidays, leave and safe and fair workplaces.

Read your employment agreement and ask questions about anything you do not understand before agreeing to it.

You can seek advice or support from someone you trust.

7. Protect Yourself Online

Starting work may involve emails, online applications, payroll information and communication with employers.

Never give an employer or another worker your JobsNZ password, PIN or two-step verification code.

Do not place passport numbers, driver licence numbers, IRD numbers or banking login information in your public JobsNZ profile.

If something about a job offer, message or request for information does not feel right, ask someone you trust or contact JobsNZ before providing sensitive information.
